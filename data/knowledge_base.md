# Renewal And Support Knowledge Base

## Enterprise Renewal Rules

Enterprise customers with unresolved P1 tickets must be escalated to the Customer Success Manager before renewal outreach. The account owner should not send a generic renewal reminder until the P1 ticket has an owner and next action.

If an enterprise customer has unpaid invoices and an open P1 ticket, the unpaid invoice alone should not be treated as churn evidence. The support incident may explain delayed payment.

## Refund Policy

Standard customers can receive usage-based credits when monthly active usage is below 20 percent of the contracted baseline for two consecutive months.

Enterprise refunds require approval from Finance and Customer Success. A refund should not be promised while an unresolved P1 incident is still open.

## Product Usage Signals

A usage drop greater than 50 percent in the last 14 days is a risk signal only when paired with at least one of the following: unresolved support tickets, unpaid invoices, failed onboarding, or renewal within 45 days.

Usage spikes after a billing reminder can indicate normal recovery. The analyst should check support history before marking the account as saved.

## Support Ticket Severity

P1 means the customer cannot use a critical workflow. P2 means a major workflow is degraded but a workaround exists. P3 means normal support.

Open P1 tickets should be cited in any revenue-risk answer. Closed tickets should not be used as active risk evidence unless the customer reopened the issue.

## Data Quality Rules

Do not infer churn from invoice status alone. Do not infer product satisfaction from usage alone. If sources disagree, explain the disagreement and mark the answer for human review.

When the CRM note conflicts with product usage or ticket data, the agent should say that the account needs manual validation instead of forcing a confident answer.
